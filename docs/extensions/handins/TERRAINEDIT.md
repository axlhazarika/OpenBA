# TERRAINEDIT hand-in (proof of concept)

`ext.terrain_edit`: a brush, started from a Terrain page in the extension window, that reshapes the ground under the mouse while a game runs. One class B toggle, **off by default**, locked for the game, `formatVersion` 1, no state of its own. Five brushes (raise, lower, flatten, mountain, smooth) with a size and a strength set on the page, and a resource tool that plants trees, plants, rocks and ore.

## 1. What it does

With the row on, the extension window gets a **Terrain** tab, top-aligned like the Foods page: in three sections. **Terrain Brushes:** a row of five picture buttons (Raise, Lower, Flatten, Mountain, Smooth: the game's own toolbar buttons with a glyph, its name as a tool tip) and a **Strength** slider. **Place Resources:** the resource tool, below. **Both Tools:** the **Size** slider, an **Undo** button with a note that it reverses the last stroke of either tool, and a status line (`Flatten brush, 30 to undo`). A tool button picks the tool and installs the brush as the player's current tool; the button of the tool in use stays lit, as the game's toolbar leaves its selected tool lit (the buttons are check boxes whose check mark is the white glyph; all go out when the brush stops). The sliders are read once a frame while the brush is the active tool, so the host needs no slider event. While the left mouse button is held over the map, each frame paints the mode under a disc of cells around the mouse; right click ends the tool and the game's own tool manager reinstalls its default tool, as it does for every tool. The brush draws its disc as a field of tiles under the mouse, tinted by mode, as the game's removal tool draws its selection. Size runs 1 to 24 cells in steps of 2 (6 at the start), strength 0.5 to 4 in steps of 0.5 (1 at the start).

| mode | what a painted frame does to a vertex of the disc |
|---|---|
| Raise | adds 0.04 x strength x the disc's falloff to the height, up to a ceiling of 15 (the game draws its terrain chunks between -16 and +16, so ground above that would drop out of the picture); ground already above the ceiling is left alone |
| Lower | subtracts the same, down to a floor of -3 (under the game's deep-water line, -1.4); ground that is already below the floor is left alone. Ground taken below -0.75 becomes water: see Water below |
| Flatten | moves the height toward one target, the height of the vertex under the mouse when the stroke began, held between -0.2 and 0.7 (the band the game builds on is -0.25 to 0.75); 12% of the way per frame at the centre, x strength |
| Mountain | moves the height toward a cone with a pointed top (`MountainHeight`): the peak is 5.5 + 2.4 x strength (6.7 to 15.1, never above 15: the game draws its terrain chunks between -16 and +16, and ground it generates is mountains of that order; the game calls 2.0 and up a mountain), falling to 0 at the brush's radius with an exponent of 1.6, roughened by smooth value noise of up to 30% that is fixed for a stroke (a seed taken from where the stroke started); 12% of the way per frame at the centre, x strength. Ground already higher than the aim is left alone |
| Smooth | moves the height toward the average of the vertex's eight neighbours, read before any vertex of the frame is written (so the order the vertices are visited in does not matter); 35% of the way per frame at the centre, x strength; spikes come down and pits come up, never past the average |

Per painted frame:

1. The height words (`WorldGeometry::_heights`, int16, world height = word / 256) of the disc's vertices change by the mode's rule above, weighted by a smooth bump (`terrain_edit_math.h`, `Falloff`, `NextWord`). A cell that has a blocker (a building) is left alone.
2. Each touched vertex's chunk is marked dirty (`WorldGeometry::MarkChunksDirty`); the game's own `RebuildDirtyChunks` refills and re-uploads the chunk, normals and decals included, as for the quarry.
3. `StillTerrain::Bump()`, the terrain epoch the citizen z memo watches.
4. Every cell of the disc, and two cells beyond it (a cell's slope reads its neighbours' heights), that holds a natural terrain type (2, 6, 7, 8, 10) is classified again from its new height and slope (`Map::ClassifyTerrain`) and set through `Map::SetTerrainType`, which updates the walkable zones, the region graph and the pathfinder thread. A cell raised high enough becomes type 6, a mountain, which no citizen walks on; a cell flattened into the buildable band becomes type 2, which can be built on.

**Undo.** An **Undo** button on the page reverses the most recent stroke (a held left button is one stroke; the last 30 are kept). A stroke records the height word of each vertex it changed as it was before the stroke (the first record of a vertex wins, so a long stroke keeps the original and not the last value) and the type of each cell it retyped. Undo writes the words back, marks their chunks dirty, bumps the terrain epoch, sets the recorded types back and puts the trees and rocks on the cells around the restored vertices back on the ground, the way the brushes do (so no resource heights are stored). The strokes, and the cache of the water quads, belong to the world they were painted on (the map, its height words and their stride, and the lock epoch, which a new game, a load or a reload changes, so a world allocated at the addresses of the last one is still another world): after a new game or a load Undo drops them and does nothing. The page shows how many strokes can be undone. `terrain_undo.h` is the pure part.

**Redo.** A **Redo** button, next to Undo, paints again the stroke the last Undo reversed. Each stroke remembers how it was painted (the tool, the picked resource by name, and for every frame the cell under the cursor and the size, strength and density of that frame), and an Undo hands that to a redo list; a Redo paints the frames again through the same brush code (`TerrainBrushTool::Paint`, `PlantStroke`), so the heights, the water mesh, the textures and the planted resources are made by the code that made them the first time and there is no second copy to keep in step. The stroke is a new undo step, so it can be undone again, and the other strokes waiting in the redo list stay. The way forward ends the way a fork in the road does: a stroke that changes something, or another world, drops the redo list. A Redo is also refused, as an Undo is, when something has been built on the stroke's ground since: the Undo keeps the height vertices and the retyped cells the stroke changed, and a Redo is refused as a whole when one of those vertices touches a blocker (a building, a fence, a bridge) or a road, or a retyped cell is blocked or a road, because the brush leaves such vertices alone and the stroke would come out different. Building anywhere else does not matter. The status line then reads "Cannot redo: something was built on this stroke" (as "Cannot undo" does), and the strokes still waiting stay on the list, so the Redo works again if the building is removed. The page counts the strokes a Redo can reach (from the next one down to the first with something built on its ground, read when the list changes and about twice a second), and the Undo count is read the same way: from the top down to the first stroke with something built on it, since the strokes are undone in order. A planting is painted again with the same cell choice (the seed comes from the first cell) but a fresh jitter and variant for each resource, and it needs the resource still in the drop down's list. The last 30 undone strokes are kept, and the status line says how many can be redone while there are any. There is no keyboard shortcut yet.

**Water.** The water the player sees is a mesh the game builds once, at world generation (`WorldGeometry::BuildWaterMesh`): for each lake feature, a quad for every cell with a corner at or below -0.75, kept in the world's own arrays (`_contourVertices`, `_contourIndices`, the lake draw ranges `_contourRangesB`) and saved with the world. When a stroke ends (the left button goes up), the brush makes the water of the region the stroke changed again from the ground's heights now: it takes out the edit's own quads there and the game's own where all four corners of a cell are above -0.75 (the six indices of a quad are made one repeated vertex, so nothing is drawn; an undo puts them back), and builds a quad for every cell of the region that is under water and not drawn by a game quad (`terrain_water.h`, `BuildWaterPatch`, pure and unit-tested), appended as one new lake range. The z field of a water vertex is the height of its water (the mesh builder writes the feature's number minus 191, in 1/48 units, so each lake lies a hair above the one before it; the rivers use -192 and down), and two lakes whose z differ show a thin line where they meet, where the bed shows through the gap. A patch therefore takes the z of the water it joins: that of the edit's own quads in or within two cells of the region if there are any, else that of the game's nearest quad, else -191 (the first lake's). The edit's quads are told from the game's, after a load as well, by a byte the game always leaves at 0, the fourth component of a vertex's normal, which they set to 1. **Rivers.** The game draws a river as strips laid along a centre line, in the other range list (`_contourRangesA`, a different material, a faster texture flow): u runs across the channel (0 to 1), v = arc length / 32 - 16. A stroke whose region has a river triangle within a few cells of its middle (one of the game's strips, or an edit river patch) is built river-style: the same quads, appended to the river list, textured with the mapping of the river triangles around it, each vertex taking the mapping of the nearest triangle (`UvAnchor`, within 12 cells of the region; a river bends, so no one plane fits it for long, and with one plane from a single triangle the flow did not line up along the join), each mapping being u and v as linear functions of the world position (`UvMapOfTriangle` solves u and v as linear functions of the world position from the triangle's three vertices; a constant u gave one texture column stretched into streaks), at the river's own z, so the texture runs the river's way at the river's scale and its phase continues where they meet. A cell the game's river triangles already cover completely (its four corners and its centre inside them, `RiverCover`) is left to the river, for a lake patch as well: two layers of water at one height z-fight. A cell the river's edge cuts through still gets a quad: leaving it out (a test on its centre alone) left a triangle of the cell with no water, dark where the bed showed through. Where the patch overlaps the game's strip there the two are at one height and would z-fight (a flicker), so a patch that follows the game's own river lies 2 / 48 of a unit higher than it; one that continues an earlier patch of the edit takes that patch's height as it is. Any other stroke is a lake. The river-style quads carry the edit marker too, and are read, taken out and undone like the lake ones (undo notes the river range count as well). A range is drawn with its own base vertex, so the mesh's 16-bit index limit applies to a range and not to the whole mesh (a large map's mesh holds about 54,000 vertices today); the draw list is built from the range count each frame, so the new range is drawn without any other change. A patch holds at most 65535 vertices. The cells the game's own quads already draw (read from the mesh once per world) are skipped, since a cell drawn twice shows as a pale patch. A corner's shore weight (the byte the game's mesh keeps in a vertex's normal) is all of it at -0.75 and below and none at 0.25 and above. The buffers are made again from the arrays (the old ones let go through the buffer's own destructor and constructor, since `Create` alone would leave the old buffer behind). Undo puts back the quads the stroke took out, cuts the three arrays back to the sizes it noted before the stroke, makes the buffers again, and drops its list of drawn cells. The undo count is checked against the world (the map, its height words and their stride) whenever the page reads it, so after a load it is 0. The cells themselves become type 7 or 8 (shallow or deep water) through the same re-classification as before, so citizens do not walk on them. A tree, rock or ore deposit on a cell that becomes type 7 or 8 is removed (it cannot stand in a lake, and left in place it would show on the lake bed through the water): the entity is marked for removal and queued, as the first step of `NaturalResourceComponent::RemoveResource` does for a resource that is not harvested, and the game's own teardown frees its cell. An undo restores the ground, the types and the water but does not bring such a resource back (the page says so).

**The town's water access.** The town keeps a list of pairs {a buildable cell that touches water, the water cell it touches} (`CitizenBehavior` +0x130), built once when a world is made (`MapTileGrid_Build`'s shore loop) and saved with the town, and a coarse index of it (+0x140: blocks of 8 x 8 cells holding the pairs' indices, and, below zero, the wells). The fire-fighting search (`CitizenBehavior::FindWater`) reads both to find the nearest water. A lake the brush digs is in neither, and a lake it drains would stay in both. When a stroke ends, and when one is undone, the pairs of the stroke's area (and three cells around it) are made again from the cell types as they are then, by the shore loop's own rule (a type 2 cell takes the first type 7 or 8 neighbour of its 3 x 3, rows then columns); a pair that touches the area from outside has its outside cell looked at again; the index is built again (its well entries, below zero, are kept). Pairs outside the area keep their order. This is the game's own saved data, so a save carries it with no format change.

**The ground texture.** The game paints the ground from a texture it builds once, when the world is generated (`WorldGeometry::_heightBytes`, uploaded as `_heightImage`; `wg_BlendTexture_cf440` and `Game/worldgen_cells.cpp`): four bytes per cell of the height grid, R the rock weight (all rock below -0.5, the lake bed and its brown shore; a patchy scatter between; ramping in above 8), G the grass weight (from the slope's normal.z, the game's thresholds 0.85 and 0.998969, and ramping in above 7) and B the ambient occlusion (a 21 x 21 circle of neighbours). The bytes are saved with the world. When a stroke ends, the bytes of the cells it changed are made again from the heights as they are then (`terrain_texture.h`, pure and unit-tested, the game's own formulas and constants): R for the cells whose height changed, G and B for the 8 x 8 blocks around them and two blocks out, since slope and occlusion read the neighbours; then the texture is uploaded again (`ImageBuffer::CopyToTexture`, as `UploadHeightImage` does). So ground lowered out of a mountain loses its rock, a new mountain above 8 gets its rock cap, and a dug lake gets its brown bed and shore. One difference: R's scatter is a perlin noise whose seeds the game drew at generation and does not keep, so a cell whose height changed takes a smooth value noise of its own (three octaves from a lattice step of 2 cells), scaled to the mean and the standard deviation of the game's own rock bytes on ordinary ground in that world (measured from the texture at each repaint: cells between -0.4 and 7.5 high that the stroke did not change, every 5th cell each way), so its splotches are of the size and strength of the surrounding ground's; a cell whose height did not change keeps the game's byte. Undo puts back the bytes the stroke repainted (a stroke records each cell's four bytes before its first repaint) and uploads the texture again.

**The resource tool (Plant).** Under the brushes the Terrain page has a **Place Resources** section: a tree button (Plant), a drop down of resources and a **Density** slider; the Size slider sets the disc as it does for the brushes. The drop down lists the natural resources that stand on the map, by the description of each occupant that carries a `NaturalResourceComponent` (a new world, by the lock epoch and the map as the brush's other per-world state is, starts the list over; within a world each scan only adds, when the page opens and when a stroke starts, so a resource that comes onto the map later, such as a tree a forester plants that the start did not scatter, joins the list and nothing on it is ever taken off), trees first, then plants, then rocks, ore and salt, each by name (`plant_math.h`: `ResourceShortName`, `ResourceOrder`). **Where the list comes from.** It is not a list the game or this extension keeps of every resource asset: it is built from what stands on the map the player is in. At a new game that is what the chosen start condition scattered (its list of `NaturalResource*` templates is what worldgen places), so the drop down changes with the start that was picked, and after a load it is what that save holds. A template that has never been on the map in this game (a resource the start condition does not scatter and nothing has planted) is not offered, and a mod's resources appear when the map holds them; one that was on the map stays on the list even after every one of it is gone. **The names** are the internal file names of the templates (`NaturalResourceWillowTree.rsc` is shown as `WillowTree`, with the folder, the extension and the `NaturalResource` prefix taken off), because the game has no string table of display names for natural resources. Choosing one selects the Plant tool. The density is how full the disc ends up. While the left button is held, the disc's eligible cells are counted (the free ones, and those a natural resource of any kind already stands on); the stroke asks for `PlantTarget` of them, the density's share rounded up, to hold a resource, and plants on enough free ones (`PickCells`: the lowest hash of the cell and the stroke's seed) to bring the disc up to it, so what already stands counts, a click on ground that is full enough plants nothing, a held button stops adding once the disc is full, and repeated clicks over one place never go past the density. A chosen cell gets the picked resource: the entity is made from the template (`ComponentEntity(desc)`), placed with `NaturalResourceComponent::PlaceAtCell` (the jittered position and yaw of worldgen), given a model variant when worldgen would give one (the description's `_b48` is set and the model has mesh groups; a group and a mesh are drawn at random) and registered, as `StartTown_SpawnResources` and the game's own `CreateNaturalResource` action do (the action needs an orchard parent, so it is not called). A cell takes one when nothing stands on it, no road is on it (`field_0x2A`) and it is ordinary land (type 2 or 10, not a mountain or water); the height band and slope limits of the template's scatter description (`NaturalResourceDescription` `_f3c`, `_f40`, `_f44`) are not applied, so a resource can be put where worldgen would not have scattered it. A plant is full size when it is placed and has its whole adult life ahead of it: `GrowthComponent::SetProgress` takes a fraction of the plant's whole life (growth = fraction x `growthScale`, and the plant dies when its growth passes `growthScale`, `GrowthComponent::Update`), so the fraction is `1 / growthScale`, not 1 (a fraction of 1 is the end of life and the plant dies at the first update; a template that never dies has `growthScale` -1, and `1 / growthScale` gives growth 1.0 there too); it then spawns saplings and the other sub-resources as any grown one does. Rocks and ore have no `GrowthComponent` and skip that step; they get a mesh variant only if their description has `_b48` set, as at worldgen. Resources are ordinary entities, so the save carries them with no format change. **Undo** removes what the last stroke planted, as long as each is still standing on the cell where it was put (one that has been chopped, mined or replaced is left alone), by the removal the terrain brushes use for a lake (`MarkForRemoval` and the removal list). There is no Erase mode yet.

## 2. Why class B

Play diverges: the map is no longer the generated one. Nothing of the row is recorded: a save already carries the heights, the per-cell terrain types and the splat bytes (`WorldGeometry::Serialize`, `Map::Serialize`), so the edits round-trip with no format change. The faithful profile turns the row off.

## 4. Not done yet (the road to the real editor)

* Things that read water cells besides the pathfinder: the fire-fighting water search (the town's water access list, rebuilt after a stroke), fishing docks and the Trading Post were tried in a game on water the brush made, and worked. The Trading Post and the fishing dock place, and their placement checks read the Water and DeepWater tiles; the trader boat found its way along a river made entirely with the brush to a Trading Post on it.
* Things already on the ground: nothing tells buildings or trees that the ground moved. A vertex that is a corner of a cell with a blocker (a building, a field) or a road (the game marks road cells in `MapCell::field_0x2A`; the road mesh is built once from the ground heights of the time, so ground moved under a road would bury it) is left alone by a stroke, and a stroke does not retype such a cell. Undo is refused as a whole (the page says "Cannot undo: something was built on this stroke", and the stroke stays on the stack) when any vertex it would restore touches such a cell, or any cell it would retype is blocked, is a road or no longer holds the type the stroke gave it: a part of an Undo would leave the water mesh and the ground texture, which it cuts back and restores whole, out of step with the heights that stayed.
* The water mesh only grows: every stroke that changes water appends a draw range, and the quads it takes out stay in the arrays as degenerate indices (all saved with the world). The game draws a range by a key of its number with 0x8000 (lake) or 0x6000 (river) added and decodes it with `& 0xE000`, so a list of 0x2000 ranges would make a lake key read as another kind. A stroke that would append a range past 0x2000 of its kind leaves the water as it was (logged); the terrain it changed still changes, so cells it dug under the water line are retyped to water and enter the town's water-access list with no water drawn there.
* The terrain chunks are culled in a fixed height range of -16 to +16.
* The resource tool does not apply a template's scatter limits (height band, slope), offers only the templates that stand on the map, plants only full-size resources (no seedlings), and has no Erase mode; Undo cannot bring back a resource that was removed by anything but Undo.

## 5. Tests

`ext_terrain_edit_test.cpp`: the registry row (class B, OFF, locked, format 1, the settings key and the faithful profile), the falloff (a smooth bump, 0 outside the radius), the disc (centred, clipped to the map, radius clamped), the height-word arithmetic (scaled by 256, clamped to int16), the size and strength steps (stay in range), raise and lower (lower stops at the floor above the water line and never raises ground below it), flatten (converges to its target, the target held inside the buildable band, no weight no change) and mountain (rises past the mountain line, never lowers ground that is already higher). The undo stack: a stroke keeps the first value of a vertex, an empty stroke is not a step, last in first out, the oldest is dropped past the limit, and clearing empties it. The toolbar and resource tests count the page and its objects. The tool itself needs the running game.

`ext_terrain_edit_test.cpp` also checks the resource tool's pure part: the short names (a folder, `.rsc:resource` and the `NaturalResource` prefix taken off), the list order (trees, plants, rocks and ore, each by name), the density target (the share of the eligible cells, rounded up, 0 when nothing is eligible), the cells a stroke picks (exactly the number asked for, the same ones for the same seed, other ones for another seed, no cell twice) and the undo record of planted resources (kept in order, and a stroke that only planted is a step).

## 6. Verified

Built and tested on Windows (MinGW) on `main` c6ad0e7 (with the Vulkan backend and the phone menu merged) plus this branch's commits (the first, rebased, is the resource tool): unit 1477 / 0 failures / 8 skipped (the kit skips); `tools/abi_audit.py` FAILs on one line in `src/render` (`long`, 5 against a baseline of 1) that `main` has too and this branch does not touch; `tools/acceptance/acceptance.sh` 0 lines off target (R14 to R16 need a new-game log directory and were not run); `OpenBA.exe` builds. `resave_gate.sh Gate` was not run by the author (it launches the game).

Looked at in a running game by the author (Windows), on the page's layout (top-aligned, the picture buttons, the two sliders, the status line) and on the tools themselves. All five brushes (raise, lower, flatten, mountain, smooth) and Undo were run in a game: lower removes a tree or rock on a cell that becomes water; a lake and a mountain made with the brushes survive a save and a load; after a load Undo does nothing, as designed; the grass, mountain, shore and water textures appear at the right time, but only when a stroke ends (the left button goes up), not while the brush is painting: during a stroke the ground rises and falls with its old texture and water; the new water works for fire fighting, for fishing docks and for the Trading Post: both place on it (their checks read Water and DeepWater tiles) and the trader boat sailed a river made entirely with the brush. The same page was looked at next to the Foods and Priority pages.

The resource tool and the lit tool buttons were looked at in a running game by the author (Windows): the Resource drop down opens and lists every template on the map (32 on the map tried), and choosing one lights the Plant button; trees, plants, rocks and ore each plant and look right (rocks and ore have no growth component and are placed without that step, and show different mesh variants); a planted tree stays alive for years at high speed, and so do planted reeds, which never die (several years at high speed, none lost), spawns saplings and the other forest resources, and is chopped for wood; density works as coverage (clicking the same place again, or one cell over, adds only what the area is short of, and a drag fills new ground); roads and buildings are not planted over; Undo removes what the last stroke planted and leaves a resource that was chopped alone; the planted resources survive a save and a load; the five brushes and their Undo are unchanged; the button of the tool in use stays lit (the game's own toolbar buttons) and goes out when the brush stops. A resource that came onto the map during a game (Palm Trees planted by a Palm Tree forester on a map without them) appeared in the drop down at the next stroke or page open, and nothing already listed went away; a list that is open at that moment does not change until it is closed and opened again. Picking a resource lights Plant from a freshly opened window, including the resource the drop down already showed; opening the list and clicking away selects nothing (the window host passes a combo box's pick, message 0x14 from `ComboDialog`, to the page and not the click that opens the list). Not run: a seedling or mixed-age planting (the tool plants only full-size resources) and the Windows device-lost case.

**Window height.** The extension window is as tall as its tallest tab, so a shorter page (this one, Foods) leaves empty space below it. The maintainer ruled in #37 that the fixed height stays.
